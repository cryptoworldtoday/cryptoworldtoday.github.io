Coinbase trust charter takes effect, expanding federal crypto custody

Coinbase National Trust took effect under OCC oversight on Nov. 6, shifting its custody business from conditional approval to a federal charter without granting deposit-taking powers.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 9, 2026 · 13:57 UTC3 min read

Coinbase National Trust Company’s national trust bank charter took effect on Nov. 6, moving the exchange’s custody operation from conditional approval to an active federal charter. The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency’s charter record lists the application as approved on April 2 and consummated/effective on Nov. 6; Coinbase announced the final approval on Nov. 9. The change gives the trust company a federal supervisory framework for its permitted activities, but does not turn it into a full-service bank.

What can Coinbase do under the charter?

The charter authorizes a national trust company focused on custody and related trust activities. In its April decision, the OCC said Coinbase’s proposed fiduciary cryptocurrency custody was permissible and that the company could help custody customers access affiliate services, including staking and financing. The OCC also required the bank to limit operations to those in its business plan and not meet the Bank Holding Company Act’s definition of a bank. Coinbase’s public application described the proposed entity as non-insured.

That scope matters. CoinDesk reported that the trust bank cannot take deposits or make loans as a full-service bank would. The charter therefore addresses how Coinbase can hold and administer customer assets under federal oversight; it does not confer the broader powers or deposit protections associated with a conventional insured bank. The OCC’s decision also says Coinbase did not propose to issue stablecoins through this entity.

How does this change Coinbase’s position?

Before the federal charter, Coinbase’s custody structure included a New York limited-purpose trust charter. Coinbase’s announcement says its custody company received New York approval in 2018, following Coinbase Inc.’s BitLicense in 2017. The federal charter places the national trust company’s trust activities within a federal framework, while building on that state-regulated history. Coinbase says the move is intended to provide a more consistent federal framework for its custody business.

The sequence took more than a year: the OCC received the application on Oct. 3, 2025, gave preliminary conditional approval on April 2, 2026, and recorded the charter as effective Nov. 6. The OCC decision notes that it received five comments on the application, including objections from trade groups representing banks and community groups. The regulator concluded the proposed activities were permissible under its authority to charter national trust banks.

Coinbase is not the only crypto firm seeking this route. CoinDesk reported that Ripple had also received a full trust charter, while other companies pursued provisional approvals. That puts Coinbase’s development in a wider shift toward federally chartered crypto custody, but the legal status of each firm depends on its own application, permitted activities and regulatory conditions.

What should customers and competitors watch next?

The immediate test is how Coinbase brings custody activity into the chartered entity and what services it offers under that structure. The company says the approval will let it expand its custody business; its April application described plans involving segregated custody accounts and access to services through affiliates. Those plans are not evidence that every proposed service is already live under the charter.

Another signal is whether federal oversight makes institutional custody easier to use across state lines, or whether customers continue to weigh a national trust charter against state-chartered providers and other custody arrangements. The trade-off is clearer federal supervision and a defined trust-company remit, alongside limits that keep this entity outside ordinary deposit-taking and lending. OCC supervision, the boundaries of the bank’s actual services and any further charter approvals will show how consequential the shift becomes.

Visa takes stablecoin prefunding pilots into Asia-Pacific

Visa will pilot stablecoin prefunding for Asia-Pacific payouts with dtcpay, letting eligible businesses fund transfers digitally while recipients continue to receive fiat.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 21, 2026 · 16:38 UTC3 min read

Visa plans to let eligible clients in Asia-Pacific fund cross-border payouts through Visa Direct with stablecoins, while recipients continue to receive fiat currency. The pilot with Singapore-based dtcpay extends a service that entered commercial production in the United States last month. Visa set out the regional plan in its Asia-Pacific announcement.

How will stablecoins fund Visa Direct payouts?

Under the pilot, dtcpay will use supported stablecoins to prefund cross-border fiat payouts through Visa Direct. Visa says independently provided digital-asset infrastructure will connect the stablecoin funding to its network; it does not itself provide regulated digital-asset services. The announcement does not identify the supported stablecoins or name the licensed infrastructure partner.

The distinction is in the funding leg, not the recipient’s payout. A business can provide stablecoins to fund a transfer, while the person receiving it gets fiat. Visa says its network connects more than 18 billion endpoints, but that figure describes the network’s reach, not the number of people or payout locations covered by this pilot. Availability will vary by client eligibility and geography.

Who is affected by the regional pilot?

The immediate participants are eligible Visa Direct clients, dtcpay and the licensed partner providing digital-asset services. Businesses that make cross-border payouts may gain another way to manage the funds available for those payments. Recipients are not being asked to receive stablecoins under the arrangement described by Visa; their payout remains in fiat.

The regional step follows earlier experiments and a US launch. Visa says it began stablecoin prefunding and payout pilots in 2025, then moved stablecoin-enabled prefunding into commercial production in the United States with Inyo Global in October 2026. The Block’s report on the Asia-Pacific expansion also describes the Inyo launch as Visa Direct’s first commercial rollout. The dtcpay effort is a pilot, so it does not establish broad regional availability.

What changes compared with bank funding?

Visa frames stablecoin prefunding as giving clients more flexibility over short-term liquidity. For a business operating across markets and time zones, the option could change when and how it funds a payout; it does not change the fiat delivery described in this pilot. The announcement does not quantify any improvement in speed, cost or settlement time, so those benefits remain unproven here.

Conventional fiat prefunding remains the baseline alternative. Stablecoin funding adds a digital-asset step and depends on a licensed partner for the regulated services, while the payout still runs through Visa Direct. That arrangement may offer flexibility in funding, but it also makes the process dependent on partner infrastructure and on whether clients and markets are eligible. Visa has not published the pilot’s transaction volumes, supported tokens, fees or timetable for a wider rollout.

The next signals are whether dtcpay’s pilot moves into regular service, which stablecoins and markets become available, and whether Visa adds clients or partners across the region. Those details will show whether the change becomes a practical funding option at scale or remains a limited test alongside fiat prefunding.

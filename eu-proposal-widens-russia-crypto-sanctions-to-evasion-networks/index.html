EU proposal widens Russia crypto sanctions to evasion networks

The Commission’s proposed 22nd Russia package would extend bank and crypto enforcement, but still needs approval from all 27 EU states before it can take effect.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 26, 2026 · 20:09 UTC3 min read

The European Commission has proposed extending its Russia sanctions to crypto and payment networks that help Moscow evade restrictions, alongside transaction bans on about 60 Russian banks. The proposal, presented on 23 November as the EU’s 22nd sanctions package, would widen pressure beyond Russia’s own financial system, according to the Associated Press report on the proposal. It is not yet in force: all 27 member states must endorse the package, a process that can take weeks and may change the measures.

The proposal puts crypto networks and evasion routes in scope

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the package would target crypto and payment networks in Russia and in third countries that help Russia evade sanctions. The Commission has also launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support that effort, according to von der Leyen’s statement on the 22nd package.

The statement does not name crypto platforms, specify which third countries or describe the proposed restrictions in operational detail. That leaves the scope of the crypto measures unresolved at this proposal stage. The stated direction, however, is broader than targeting services based only in Russia: it would also reach outside the bloc’s borders when networks are used to bypass existing restrictions.

July’s package had already widened the crypto framework

The proposal builds on measures adopted in July. The EU’s 21st package extended transaction bans to 14 crypto-related service platforms based in Georgia, Panama, the United Arab Emirates, the Marshall Islands, Kyrgyzstan and Belarus. It also created the possibility of a full third-country ban on crypto-asset services used by Russia to evade sanctions. That was an adopted measure; the November proposal now describes a further effort against crypto and payment networks, including third-country support.

The distinction matters for crypto businesses and intermediaries. A measure against named providers can restrict dealings with those entities. A broader country-level mechanism could reach transactions with any provider covered by the ban, but the July rules described that as a possibility, and the November statement does not say how any new proposal would connect to it. The EU has not yet published, in the statement, a list of affected networks or a detailed compliance test.

Bank restrictions are the clearest numerical element

Von der Leyen said the proposal would extend transaction bans to about 60 Russian banks and remove all 60 from SWIFT, the international financial messaging system. That sits alongside the crypto provisions as a direct attempt to restrict financial channels. AP reported that the Commission expects the package to require unanimous endorsement by the member states and that measures may be modified before adoption.

For crypto firms, the trade-off is between closing routes used to circumvent sanctions and uncertainty over how broadly controls may reach legitimate providers operating across borders. The proposal’s stated focus on networks that help Russia evade sanctions gives a rationale for targeting third-country services, but the Commission has not yet set out which networks meet that test. The same gap makes it premature to treat the announcement as a new blanket prohibition on crypto activity involving any particular country.

Approval and scope are the next signals

The next concrete signal is whether member states approve the package and what changes they make during negotiations. For crypto, the key details to watch are whether the final measures identify specific platforms, define the conduct that counts as helping sanctions evasion, or use the third-country mechanism adopted in July. Until those terms are settled, the proposal signals an expanded enforcement priority, while the practical reach for crypto services remains open.

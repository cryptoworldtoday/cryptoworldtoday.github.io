Zcash NU7 Begins Routing 60% of Fees to Future Rewards

Zcash activated NU7 at block 3,543,405, cutting target block time to 25 seconds and reserving 60% of fees for future miner rewards without changing its cap.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 7, 2026 · 18:08 UTC3 min read

Zcash activated its NU7 network upgrade at block 3,543,405 on Nov. 5, putting a new fee reserve into operation and cutting the target time between blocks from 75 seconds to 25. The change sets aside 60% of each block’s transaction fees for future mining rewards; it does not reduce the fee a user pays. Unchained’s report on the activation places the block at 9:34 p.m. ET and identifies the reserve as Shielded Labs’ Network Sustainability Mechanism.

The shift changes how Zcash handles fees as block subsidies decline. Before NU7, miners received the block’s transaction fees. Now, miners retain 40%, while the rest leaves circulation and enters a public reserve balance under the consensus rules. The reserve is scheduled to begin returning gradually through block rewards at block 8,939,664, projected for 2031.

When do the reserved fees return to miners?

The reserve is a delay mechanism, not a permanent fee burn. The NU7 deployment specification sets the mainnet reissuance height at 8,939,664, after which the reserve will be paid out gradually on top of regular block rewards. Until then, miners receive a smaller share of transaction fees, while the protocol accumulates funds intended to support future rewards.

That choice addresses a long-term funding question: block subsidies fall at each halving, and transaction fees could matter more to network security over time. NU7 starts preparing for that possibility without changing Zcash’s 21 million ZEC supply cap or four-year halving schedule. It also avoids an earlier proposal to replace halvings with a smoother issuance curve; according to Unchained, coinholders did not support that design, and the selected version preserves the existing schedule.

Does the faster block target change issuance?

NU7 triples the target block frequency, but it is designed to keep the rate of new ZEC issuance over time essentially unchanged. The block reward is divided by three as the target interval falls from 75 seconds to 25, and the number of blocks between halvings is increased to preserve the schedule. The faster target can shorten the average wait for a first confirmation, though actual confirmation timing varies with block production and the requirements set by wallets, exchanges and other services.

The upgrade also adds limits on shielded activity per block. The Zcash Foundation says these limits are intended to constrain the work required to validate blocks and synchronize shielded wallets as blocks arrive more often. Its estimates put Orchard throughput at 6.6 transactions per second, up from 2.9, while reducing worst-case sync bandwidth that transaction spam could impose on light wallets by about 37%. Those are protocol capacity and workload estimates, not a promise that every wallet will see the same practical gains.

Who bears the cost of the transition?

Miners receive less of the transaction-fee revenue immediately: 40% rather than the full block’s fees, with the reserved share unavailable until reissuance begins. The tradeoff is a future source of rewards that supplements scheduled subsidies while keeping the cap and halving cadence intact. Whether the mechanism helps sustain mining over the longer term will depend in part on how the reserve grows and how much fee revenue the network generates.

NU7 also makes version 4 transactions invalid. Because Zcash’s older Sprout pool can only use that transaction format, funds remaining there became unspendable at activation; Unchained reports about 12,224 ZEC remained. The next signals are the reserve’s growth, how miners respond to their reduced fee share, and whether wallets and services adapt smoothly to the faster target and new transaction rules.

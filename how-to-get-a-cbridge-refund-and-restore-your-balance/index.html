How to get a cBridge refund and restore your balance

How to get a cBridge refund: request it in transfer history, confirm the source-chain withdrawal with your sending wallet, and check your restored balance.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 19, 2026 · 20:48 UTC5 min read

Open the refundable transfer in cBridge history and request the refund with the wallet that sent it. A failed transfer needs a separate source-chain withdrawal before the original token returns to your wallet.

Insufficient liquidity on the destination chain or slippage beyond your tolerance can send a transfer into refund status. The Celer bridge uses State Guardian Network (SGN) signatures to prepare the withdrawal; for help with the bridge itself, cBridge is the place to start, while this walkthrough covers claiming the refund.

Claim the refund from transfer history

Start when the transfer is marked for refund. Have the sending wallet available and enough of the source chain’s native gas token to submit a transaction there.

  1. Connect the sending wallet. Use the address that initiated the transfer. The refund belongs to that source-chain address, so connecting a different wallet can leave the refund absent from its history even when you know a transfer failed. Reconnect the original wallet before proceeding.
  2. Open History and select the refundable transfer. Confirm you have the right transfer by checking its source chain, token, and status. A transfer awaiting a refund is at a different stage from one still waiting for fund release on the destination chain.
  3. Click Request Refund. This starts the refund process. A failed transfer does not pay itself back automatically: cBridge must prepare the withdrawal information and obtain SGN signatures for it.
  4. Wait for Confirm Refund to appear. The confirmation action becomes available once the SGN signatures are ready. Requesting the refund and confirming it are separate actions; selecting the first does not itself return the tokens.
  5. Switch the wallet to the source chain, then confirm. The withdrawal is an on-chain transaction on the network where you sent the assets. Make sure the connected wallet is still the sending wallet and is set to that source network. Click Confirm Refund and sign the transaction in your wallet. The native gas token is needed to submit it.
  6. Wait for confirmation and verify the result. Look up the withdrawal in the source-chain explorer. Once it is confirmed, check the sending wallet’s balance for the original token on that same chain. The refund returns the asset to the source side, rather than completing the intended transfer to the destination.

Why wallet and network changes matter

The refund flow changes the transfer’s outcome from a cross-chain delivery to a withdrawal on the source chain. In a successful transfer, supported assets move to the destination through pooled liquidity or token mappings. In a failed one, insufficient destination liquidity or exceeded slippage can instead trigger a refund backed by SGN signatures. The destination balance is therefore not where to look for returned funds.

The key dependency is the original sending address. cBridge associates the refund with that source-chain address; a different connected wallet has no matching withdrawal to show. If you change accounts before confirming, switch back to the sender. If you change networks, return to the source network before signing. Both conditions must be in place for the refund transaction to be submitted from the right account and chain.

Say the same transfer is refundable, but you first connect another wallet. The refund may appear to be missing because that account did not send it. Switching back to the sending wallet restores the relevant transfer context; the rest of the process still holds: request the refund, wait for signatures, confirm on the source chain, and check the explorer and balance.

Compared with retrying the bridge transfer, claiming the refund settles the failed attempt by returning the original token on its source chain. A retry would be a new transfer decision, with its own destination liquidity and slippage conditions. First verify that the withdrawal completed and the source balance is restored; then decide whether to bridge again.

Related questions

cBridge transfer pending for hours

A pending status can mean the transfer is still waiting for SGN confirmation or for fund release on the destination chain. Those stages differ from a transfer marked for refund. Check the status in history before taking action: use Request Refund only when the transfer is eligible for that flow, and wait for Confirm Refund when signatures are still being prepared.

cBridge refund not showing in wallet

First reconnect the wallet that sent the transfer and select the source chain. The refund is tied to that address and returns on that chain, so another account or a destination-chain view can make it seem absent. After confirming the withdrawal, verify the source-chain transaction in its explorer and check the original token balance.

cBridge slippage exceeded error

Slippage tolerance limits how far the execution can vary from the transfer’s expected amount. When the tolerance is exceeded, the transfer can become refundable. Follow the refund status in history and claim it with the sending wallet; changing slippage is relevant only if you later choose to make another transfer.

More cBridge questions

How long does a cBridge refund take?

The refund is ready to confirm after SGN signatures are prepared, then the source-chain transaction needs confirmation. Check the transfer status for that first stage and the source explorer for the second; network conditions affect how quickly the on-chain confirmation appears.

Why does my cBridge transfer say waiting for fund release?

That status means the transfer is waiting for release of funds on the destination chain. It is distinct from a refund status, so check history for the transfer’s current state before requesting a withdrawal.

Can I cancel a cBridge transfer in progress?

The refund flow applies when cBridge marks a transfer for refund; it is not a general cancel button for every in-progress transfer. If the transfer is still pending, use its displayed status to determine whether it has entered the refund process.

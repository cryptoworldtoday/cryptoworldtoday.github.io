Schroders takes emerging-markets debt strategy onto Solana

Schroders and Centrifuge have launched a tokenized feeder into the asset manager’s emerging-markets bond strategy on Solana, extending onchain funds beyond cash-like debt.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 5, 2026 · 13:05 UTC3 min read

Schroders and tokenization platform Centrifuge launched a tokenized route to Schroders’ emerging-markets bond strategy on Solana on Nov. 4, extending onchain investment products beyond the cash-like debt that has dominated the market. Centrifuge’s launch announcement says this is Schroders’ first deployment on Solana; the fund initially launches on that network.

The product is the Schroder International Selection Fund Emerging Market Bond. Schroders manages the underlying strategy, which invests in government and corporate debt across emerging economies. The firm oversaw $1.1 trillion in assets as of June, according to CoinDesk’s report.

What do investors hold through the token?

Investors hold tokenized shares in a feeder fund that invests in Schroders’ Emerging Market Bond Fund, CoinDesk reports; they do not hold the underlying bonds or shares in the underlying fund directly. The share value tracks the feeder’s net asset value. This structure puts a fund interest onchain while leaving the underlying investment strategy with Schroders.

That distinction matters: tokenization changes how the feeder’s shares are represented and distributed, but it does not remove the credit, currency or economic risks of emerging-markets debt. Schroders has managed the asset class for more than 50 years and has over 40 investment professionals dedicated to it, according to CoinDesk. Its experience and active management are relevant to the underlying portfolio; neither guarantees returns or makes the token itself equivalent to cash.

How does this differ from earlier onchain bond products?

Tokenized U.S. Treasuries and money-market funds have been the larger part of the real-world asset market so far. CoinDesk, citing RWA.xyz, reported that those products accounted for $14 billion of a $39 billion tokenized RWA market. They offer exposure to short-duration government debt or cash-like assets. Emerging-markets bonds instead bring exposure to borrowers, currencies and economic conditions across developing countries, with a different set of risks and sources of return.

The move also builds on Schroders’ earlier tokenization work, rather than marking its first tokenized fund initiative. CoinDesk reported that the firm received Irish regulatory approval in August for a tokenized share class of its U.S. dollar money-market fund, Schroders Onchain Active Returns. The Solana launch shifts the asset class and blockchain: from a cash-like fund to emerging debt, and from that earlier initiative to the public Solana network.

What will show whether the move gains traction?

The launch tests whether investors and onchain applications will use a tokenized feeder for a more complex, actively managed strategy, not just cash-like products. The trade-off is clearer access through blockchain rails against exposure to more varied borrower and currency risks; tokenization does not itself establish deep liquidity or simplify the underlying credit analysis.

Signals to watch include the fund’s uptake, whether its tokenized shares become usable across additional financial applications or liquidity venues, and how the product reports its net asset value and underlying exposures. Centrifuge’s announcement says the fund initially launches on Solana, but does not provide a subscription total. Adoption and liquidity therefore remain open questions, as does whether other managers follow with strategies that carry risks beyond short-duration government debt.

Stablecoin holders: Aave or Compound for USDC lending?

Aave vs Compound for lending stablecoins: Aave lets supplied USDC serve as collateral; Compound III suits a simpler single-market supply. Compare rates and risk.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 28, 2026 · 00:38 UTC4 min read

Aave V3 and Compound III are close choices for lending USDC, but Aave suits holders who may want to borrow against their supply, while Compound III offers a simpler single-market supply. Their key difference is how supplied stablecoins fit into collateral: on Aave, eligible USDC can earn interest and support a borrow; on Compound III, USDC supplied as the market’s base asset earns interest but does not serve as collateral.

Borrowing against your USDC

Aave V3 lets you supply eligible USDC, earn interest, and enable that supply as collateral for borrowing. You can keep the option to borrow open even if you do not plan to use it right away. Collateral use depends on the asset’s settings in that market, and borrowing adds liquidation risk if your position becomes undercollateralized.

Compound III separates each market around one borrowable base asset. In a USDC-base market, supplying USDC earns interest; it does not also give you borrowing power. Borrowing in that market depends on supplying an eligible collateral asset from its collateral list. The base asset and collateral assets have different roles: only suppliers of the base asset earn interest, while collateral supports borrowing.

That distinction is useful if your plan may change. A stablecoin holder who wants to keep USDC available as borrowing collateral can use Aave, subject to the market’s collateral settings. Someone who only wants to supply USDC can compare Compound III on its supply rate and market terms. As another route, Morpho vaults are curated pools that allocate a single supplied asset across selected lending markets, adding an allocation layer to the lending choice.

Collateral exposure and market design

Aave pools multiple assets in a market, with each asset represented by its own reserve and settings. If you use USDC as collateral, your borrowing capacity and liquidation exposure depend on that reserve’s parameters, your debt, and the value of your collateral. The broader pool lets you supply and borrow across supported assets, but it also means you should review which assets you enable and how the market treats them.

Compound III isolates each base asset in its own market and defines a list of collateral assets for that market. In a USDC-base market, you are exposed to that market’s collateral configuration when borrowing, while a USDC supplier is lending into the base-asset market. Supplying base-asset USDC does not make you a borrower or collateral provider there. This separation can make the basic supply decision easier to read, but the collateral list still matters if you borrow.

On either protocol, check the specific market on the chain you intend to use. Aave and Compound deployments on Ethereum or Arbitrum can have different assets, settings, liquidity, and rates. A token’s role on one chain does not establish its role on another.

How to compare supply rates

Supply rates on both protocols move with utilization: the share of available base-asset or reserve liquidity that borrowers use. As borrowing demand changes, the rate changes too. Both protocols also use rate parameters that affect how utilization translates into supplier returns.

Compare live USDC supply rates for the same chain and the same time. A displayed rate is a snapshot, not a promise of what you will earn over your holding period. A higher rate can reflect higher utilization, but it does not settle whether the market’s collateral design or exposure fits your purpose. Check available liquidity as well, since withdrawals depend on assets being available to withdraw.

For a Morpho vault, the quoted return reflects the vault’s selected allocation and underlying markets. Read which markets it can allocate to and what risks those choices create; the curator’s selection adds another decision layer beyond supplying directly to Aave or Compound III.

Who each option suits

Aave V3 fits a stablecoin holder who wants the option to borrow against eligible USDC. The supply can earn interest and, when enabled as collateral, support borrowing. That flexibility comes with position management: borrowing against it means watching collateral value, debt, and liquidation conditions.

Compound III fits a holder who wants to supply USDC as the base asset in a single market and does not need that same supply to back a borrow. Its base-asset and collateral roles are distinct, so a USDC supplier earns interest without taking on a collateral-backed borrowing position. If you plan to borrow, you will need eligible collateral in the market.

Decide first whether you may borrow against your stablecoins. If so, choose Aave and confirm USDC’s collateral settings on your chosen chain. If you only plan to lend, compare the live supply rates and market terms on both protocols, then choose the higher rate only if you accept its collateral and market exposure.

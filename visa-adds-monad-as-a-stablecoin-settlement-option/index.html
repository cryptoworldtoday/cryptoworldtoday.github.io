Visa adds Monad as a stablecoin settlement option

Visa has added Monad as a stablecoin settlement option for participating issuers and acquirers, widening its blockchain choices as settlement volume grows.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 11, 2026 · 09:34 UTC3 min read

Visa has added the Monad blockchain as a stablecoin settlement option for participating card issuers and acquirers, giving them another network for settling obligations with Visa. The Monad Foundation’s November 10 announcement quotes Visa executive Dan Roesbery describing the addition as a way to expand partners’ options. It does not identify which stablecoins can settle on Monad or name any participating issuers or acquirers.

The change adds another route within Visa’s existing settlement program; it does not replace card payments or change what cardholders see at checkout. Settlement happens behind the scenes between financial institutions. The foundation says blockchain settlement can move funds on weekends and holidays, whereas traditional card settlement has typically run on a five-business-day banking schedule.

How does Monad compare with Visa’s other settlement networks?

Monad joins a program that already includes networks such as Ethereum, Solana, Avalanche, Stellar, Base, Polygon, Canton, Arc and Tempo, according to The Block’s report on the addition. The choice of network matters to institutions because existing custody, wallet and compliance systems can shape the cost and complexity of connecting to a settlement rail.

Monad is compatible with the Ethereum Virtual Machine, the software environment used by Ethereum applications. The foundation says firms using Ethereum-based tools can typically extend those integrations to Monad rather than build new ones. That may lower the work required to add the network, though the announcement gives no estimate of implementation cost or timing for any partner.

Monad’s stated network performance is 10,000 transactions per second, 400-millisecond block times and 800-millisecond finality. Those are foundation figures, not reported Visa settlement results. They describe the underlying network’s capacity and confirmation speed; the announcement does not say how much Visa settlement activity will run on Monad or how its performance compares in practice with other supported chains.

What does the addition change for institutions?

For issuers and acquirers, an additional network can provide another way to move stablecoin settlement funds without converting them to fiat first, according to the foundation. Ethereum compatibility may make Monad a more familiar integration for firms already using that ecosystem. But adding a chain also means institutions must decide whether its operational, custody and compliance requirements suit their own systems.

The wider program is growing. The foundation’s release says Visa reported a $25 billion annualized stablecoin settlement run rate as of September 2026, roughly 10 times the level a year earlier, and more than 225 stablecoin-linked card programs live globally. An annualized run rate is a projection from a recent pace; it is not a claim that $25 billion has already settled on Monad. The new network’s share, if any, has not been disclosed.

Privacy is another part of Monad’s pitch to institutions, but it is not available yet. The foundation says its planned Private Settlement feature, expected in the coming months, is designed to keep commercially sensitive transaction details confidential while allowing access for auditors and regulators. Visa has also explored private settlement on Canton, The Block reports, so Monad will enter a field where privacy is already part of the network comparison.

What should readers watch next?

The practical test is whether Visa partners begin using Monad and disclose which stablecoins and settlement flows they support. The timing and design of Private Settlement will also matter: the feature’s value will depend on whether institutions can meet confidentiality needs while retaining access to shared liquidity and meeting oversight requirements. Until partners or Visa publish usage figures, the addition expands the available infrastructure, but its role in actual settlement remains unmeasured.

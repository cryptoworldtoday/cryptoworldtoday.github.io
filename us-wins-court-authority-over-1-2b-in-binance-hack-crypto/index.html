US Wins Court Authority Over $1.2B in Binance Hack Crypto

The Justice Department secured court authority over more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack proceeds, part of $2.7 billion frozen by public and private efforts.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 13, 2026 · 11:59 UTC3 min read

The Justice Department has obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a legal step affecting a portion of the funds frozen so far rather than a final transfer to victims. In its November 12 announcement, the department said public and private efforts had frozen more than $2.7 billion of assets from a theft of more than $6 billion.

The scale is large, but the stages matter. The FBI says the theft occurred on or about October 29 and attributes it to North Korean actors it calls TraderTraitor. The FBI’s October 31 cyber alert said the actors were converting the stolen assets primarily into Ether, with further laundering through decentralised finance and instant swap services expected. The Justice Department says tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts are continuing as the funds move through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges.

The court-authorised amount is less than the total already frozen: more than $1.2 billion within a pool exceeding $2.7 billion. Against the reported theft of more than $6 billion, the authorised portion is also only part of the alleged proceeds. The figures describe different steps in the recovery process. A freeze can prevent an asset from moving while authorities pursue legal control; seizure or restraint authority gives the government a further route to seek that control. Neither figure establishes how much has been finally forfeited or returned.

The distinction is consequential for any eventual recovery. The Associated Press reported that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. That leaves a legal process between the current court authority and any distribution. The Justice Department has said it intends to seize and return North Korea’s ill-gotten gains, but its announcement does not specify a timetable for forfeiture or identify recipients.

For now, the response combines government legal process with private-sector intervention. The Justice Department credited virtual asset service providers with helping to freeze the funds, while the FBI and its partners have urged exchanges, DeFi services, instant exchanges and other providers to block transactions linked to identified addresses. That cooperation can interrupt movement across services, while ongoing laundering attempts mean the pool of assets under control may change as investigators trace transfers.

The next signals are whether the government files forfeiture claims, how much of the frozen total remains accessible as tracing continues, and whether court-authorised control expands beyond the current amount. The FBI has published addresses associated with TraderTraitor and is asking service providers to block related transactions. Those measures show an active effort to contain and recover the funds; they do not yet settle the final recoverable amount or who will receive it.

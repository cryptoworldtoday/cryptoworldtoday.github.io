World Bank explores USDC payments with Citi and Circle

World Bank will explore USDC payments with Citi and Circle, assessing whether stablecoin rails can improve development disbursements alongside correspondent banking.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 20, 2026 · 02:39 UTC3 min read

The World Bank Group will explore using regulated US dollar stablecoins for development fund payments with Citi and Circle, assessing whether blockchain based rails can improve on correspondent banking. The World Bank’s November 17 announcement describes an exploration, not a switch to stablecoin payments or a confirmed rollout. It says the group will examine whether the new model can work alongside or improve existing disbursement arrangements.

The division of work links traditional banking infrastructure with a stablecoin settlement layer. Citi will bring connections to established banking rails, while Circle will provide payments infrastructure to mint and settle funds in USDC. The World Bank says the initial model will be intermediated and interoperable. That keeps banks in the payment path while the partners assess how a digital dollar might fit into the existing system.

This is a narrower step than replacing correspondent banking. In that established arrangement, banks pass cross-border payments between institutions; the proposed exploration would test blockchain based rails alongside that route. The World Bank has not published projected savings, transfer times or a transaction volume for the initiative, so its potential cost and speed gains remain questions to measure. The Block’s report on the announcement likewise describes the effort as an exploration of regulated dollar stablecoins, rather than a live disbursement service.

FundsChain provides an existing transparency layer

The work builds on FundsChain, the World Bank’s blockchain platform for tracking development fund disbursements. According to the group, more than 350 projects across 96 countries are onboarded to its digital ledgers, where project participants can see how funds are used. That gives the stablecoin exploration an existing traceability system to build on, but tracking a disbursement and changing how money settles are separate functions. The announcement does not say FundsChain itself will mint or hold USDC.

The scale figures help define what is at stake, but they do not indicate how much funding would move through USDC. The World Bank has not named participating projects, countries or payment amounts. Nor has it announced a launch date or a target for comparing the stablecoin route with correspondent banking. The initial exploration will involve borrower countries only where digital asset use is supported and permitted, a condition that limits where the model could be applied.

Permission and reach shape the next test

Using USDC could offer another settlement route while retaining Citi’s banking connections. But an intermediated model still depends on the institutions and rails around it; interoperability across systems and local permission are practical constraints, not details the announcement resolves. The World Bank says it will explore working with other banks over time to broaden the initiative, suggesting the initial arrangement is intended to assess a model before any wider reach is established.

The next signals are whether the partners identify a live payment flow, disclose participating borrower countries, and publish comparable figures for cost, settlement time and traceability against current arrangements. Until those details emerge, the development is an institutional test of how regulated stablecoins might fit into development finance, not evidence that they have displaced correspondent banking.

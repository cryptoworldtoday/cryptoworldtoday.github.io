Ethlabs calls for Ethereum quantum defenses by 2027

Ethlabs wants Ethereum to reach post-quantum readiness in 2027, a year ahead of its cited 2028 target, trading near-term features for earlier protection.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 12, 2026 · 18:02 UTC4 min read

Ethlabs has proposed making post-quantum security a top Ethereum protocol priority for 2027, arguing that AI is making the timing of cryptographic and software risks harder to predict. In its November 11 proposal on the Ethereum Magicians forum, the research group calls the plan “pqai27” and asks developers to reshape upcoming work around it. The proposal has not been adopted into Ethereum’s roadmap.

The shift would bring the target forward by a year from the 2028 goal Ethlabs says the Ethereum Foundation had set, and by two years from the Foundation’s earlier December 2029 target, as reported by CoinDesk. Ethlabs says AI adds urgency because it could accelerate software vulnerability discovery and research into mathematical problems relevant to cryptography. The group also acknowledges that nobody knows whether AI will uncover a break in cryptographic assumptions.

What would Ethereum need to deliver by 2027?

Ethlabs separates the goal into a readiness stage and a more demanding full-capacity stage. “Pqai-ready” would mean a post-quantum consensus layer and a post-quantum route for every user’s account, while the network continues at the scaled capacity delivered by the planned Glamsterdam upgrade. Ethlabs says Ethereum could keep running at reduced capacity if today’s cryptography broke sooner than expected.

“Pqai-scaled” would support post-quantum transactions and data availability at full capacity. Ethlabs labels this a stretch goal for 2027: post-quantum signatures take up more transaction space, so users could face higher costs before signature aggregation and post-quantum data-availability technology allow transactions to run at ordinary scale. Its proposal sketches Frames, which would let accounts use different authentication methods, as part of the account migration path.

That distinction matters for users: protocol changes alone would not move existing accounts to post-quantum signing. Ethlabs says an orderly migration would also require wallet and other tooling support. The proposal’s readiness target is therefore a route to switch, not a claim that every account would already have switched.

Which Hegotá features could move aside?

Ethlabs says Hegotá, the planned 2027 hard fork, should prioritize the post-quantum work. It will withdraw its “quick slots” proposal from the fork and redirect its resources to pqai27, while saying it still supports faster slots in 2028. Quick slots sought faster block production; withdrawing them gives up that near-term change to make room for security work.

The group also says FOCIL, a planned feature intended to make transaction censorship harder, should be scheduled only if it does not put the 2027 target at risk. Ethlabs argues that delaying FOCIL could let Frames progress earlier without waiting for consensus-layer work. This is a sequencing trade-off: earlier security and account migration would take priority, while some speed and censorship-resistance work could arrive later. Ethlabs says Glamsterdam and the scaling it enables would proceed as planned.

What has to happen before the target becomes a roadmap?

Core developers still need to assess whether the timeline is achievable and decide how Hegotá’s scope changes. Ethlabs identifies the choice between adapting the existing Gasper consensus protocol and moving to decoupled consensus as an open question. It also says signature aggregation and post-quantum data availability are needed for the full-capacity stage. These are substantial protocol and coordination tasks, not just a change to account signatures.

For now, the comparison is between Ethlabs’ proposed 2027 priority and the 2028 target it cites for the Foundation—not between two approved delivery dates. The signals to watch are whether core developers accept the earlier deadline, what Hegotá retains or defers, and whether wallet migration and post-quantum scaling can keep pace with the protocol work. CoinDesk’s report on the proposal notes that wallet providers would need to update their software and holders would still have to switch signing methods.

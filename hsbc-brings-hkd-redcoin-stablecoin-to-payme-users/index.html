HSBC brings HKD RedCoin stablecoin to PayMe users

HSBC has launched its HKD stablecoin on PayMe, starting with transfers and merchant payments as its planned bank app and tokenised investment uses follow later.

By The Coin Wire EditorialOct 24, 2026 · 08:01 UTC4 min read

HSBC launched its first Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, RedCoin (RCHKD), on PayMe on 23 October, bringing a regulated bank-issued token to a retail payments app with more than 3.3 million users. HSBC’s launch announcement says users will be able to activate wallets in the coming days for peer-to-peer transfers and payments at more than 100,000 merchants. The bank says it received its Hong Kong Monetary Authority issuer licence in April.

The launch moves HSBC’s plan from the licensing stage to a consumer product, but the initial offer is contained within PayMe. HSBC says it will add wallet functionality to its Hong Kong mobile banking app later this year and plans to let customers subscribe to tokenised investment products through RedCoin, including a forthcoming share class of its Hong Kong dollar money market fund.

What can PayMe users do with RedCoin now?

At launch, RedCoin is for transfers between people and payments to merchants through PayMe. HSBC says more than 3.3 million users will be able to activate a stablecoin wallet in the app, while the bank’s staff pilot involved nearly 100 people testing the journey from minting and wallet activation through live transfers.

The bank’s earlier April plan included peer-to-peer payments, merchant payments and tokenised investments, but those uses are arriving in stages. The mobile banking app and investment features are future plans, not launch functions. HSBC says custody for RedCoin is provided by HSBC Digital Assets Custody. Its announcement identifies the token as HKD-denominated and licensed and regulated by the HKMA; the bank’s April announcement said the planned stablecoin would be fully backed at all times by high-quality liquid assets held in segregated accounts.

How does RedCoin compare with HSBC’s earlier digital products?

RedCoin extends HSBC’s Hong Kong digital asset activity into retail payments. The bank already offers its Gold Token to retail customers, digital bonds through HSBC Orion for institutional investors, and a tokenised deposit service for wholesale payments. Those products serve different markets or functions; RedCoin’s distinguishing initial use is paying people and merchants from a consumer app.

That makes the PayMe experience central to the test. The South China Morning Post’s launch report says users can select the wallet after updating the app, with no minimum balance or additional account-opening requirement, and that the interface is designed to remain consistent with PayMe’s current experience. That may lower the friction of trying the token. It also means the practical difference from existing PayMe transfers will depend on what RedCoin enables beyond the familiar app flow.

The closest local alternative is at a different stage. Ledger Insights reported that Anchorpoint, a joint venture involving Standard Chartered, HKT and Animoca Brands, began a phased institutional rollout of its HKDAP stablecoin in August, initially limited to institutional and professional investors. RedCoin therefore brings a retail route into Hong Kong’s licensed stablecoin market, while its initial access remains inside HSBC’s own platform.

What will show whether the launch changes how people pay?

The next milestones are HSBC’s planned mobile banking wallet and tokenised investment subscriptions, followed by the wholesale, corporate and institutional uses that HSBC says it intends to pursue. The money market fund share class will show whether the token can connect payments with investment products, rather than remaining another way to make transfers within PayMe.

For now, the clearest measures will be whether PayMe users activate wallets, make repeat transfers and use RedCoin at merchants. HSBC has announced a large potential user base and broad merchant access, but its launch materials do not report adoption or transaction volumes. Those figures, alongside details of redemption and how the token works outside HSBC’s apps, will determine how much the stablecoin adds to the payment network already in place.

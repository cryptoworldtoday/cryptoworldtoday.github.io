SparkLend borrowing modes: Which one fits your loan?

SparkLend borrowing modes compared: standard keeps borrowing flexible, E-Mode boosts correlated assets, and isolation caps debt against riskier collateral.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 27, 2026 · 16:26 UTC5 min read

After you choose collateral for a loan, SparkLend’s borrowing mode determines whether you keep broad borrowing options, get more power from correlated assets, or accept a shared cap for riskier collateral. Standard mode offers flexibility, E-Mode favors assets in one category, and isolation mode limits debt against an isolated asset.

  • Choose standard when you need to mix collateral or borrow assets across categories.
  • Choose E-Mode when collateral and debt are correlated and you want the category’s higher LTV and liquidation threshold.
  • Use isolation mode when your collateral is an isolated asset and its approved borrow assets and debt ceiling suit the loan.

How do the three SparkLend borrowing modes compare?

Each mode sets a different balance between borrowing power, asset choice, and risk limits. In standard mode, each collateral asset uses its own loan-to-value ratio (LTV) and liquidation threshold, and you can borrow any asset enabled for borrowing. You retain the most room to combine collateral and debt, while borrowing power follows the standard parameters for each asset.

E-Mode applies category-specific parameters when your collateral and debt belong to the same category. For ETH-correlated assets, that can mean a higher LTV and liquidation threshold, allowing more borrowing against that collateral. The trade-off is that your debt must stay within the category’s permitted assets. Aave’s official E-Mode and Isolation Mode FAQ explains these category and borrowing restrictions.

Isolation mode is designed for collateral that carries a tighter risk limit. When you supply an isolated asset as collateral, you can borrow only assets approved for borrowing in isolation, typically stablecoins, and total debt against that collateral is subject to its debt ceiling. SparkLend uses Aave V3 code; a SparkLend deployment verification documents the shared Aave V3 basis and the ETH-correlated E-Mode setup for WETH and wstETH.

That makes the choice concrete: wstETH collateral with a WETH loan fits the ETH-correlated E-Mode category. A newly listed isolated token backing a DAI loan falls under isolation mode, provided DAI is approved for borrowing against it. The feature described at SparkLend is a practical example of a permissionless lending market where borrowers can use listed collateral to borrow other listed assets.

What changes in borrowing power and risk?

Standard mode makes room for mixed debts, but each collateral contributes borrowing power under its own parameters. E-Mode can increase borrowing power for correlated collateral and debt, though the category restriction narrows your choices. Isolation mode permits riskier collateral to support a loan while limiting the eligible debt assets and capping protocol-wide exposure.

The debt ceiling is shared by all users of an isolated asset. That detail can matter even when your individual position looks healthy: your borrow may fail once other users have filled the global ceiling, although your own health factor remains fine. A sound health factor measures the safety of your position against liquidation; it does not reserve room under the shared ceiling.

So an isolated asset creates two separate constraints. Your own collateral and debt determine your health factor, while aggregate borrowing against that isolated asset determines whether capacity remains under the ceiling. A user who checks only personal collateral can mistake the global limit for a position-specific limit and plan a loan that cannot be opened.

E-Mode and isolation also solve different problems. E-Mode increases efficiency when asset prices are correlated; isolation limits how much debt the market can take against a riskier asset. Treating them as interchangeable can lead to the wrong move: trying to borrow an unrelated asset in E-Mode, or assuming an isolated asset supports any debt up to a healthy personal borrowing limit.

Which mode should you choose?

Choose standard mode when you need mixed collateral or debts that do not fit one E-Mode category. Choose E-Mode when your collateral and intended loan are correlated, such as wstETH and WETH, and the category’s eligible assets meet your needs. Choose isolation mode when the collateral is marked isolated and you can work within its approved borrow list and shared debt ceiling.

Before committing to a loan, watch the parameters that can change the decision: which assets belong to an E-Mode category, which debts are allowed in isolation, and how much debt capacity remains under an isolated asset’s ceiling. Those settings determine whether the borrowing power or flexibility you expect is actually available.

Related

How do I turn on E-Mode in SparkLend?

E-Mode is a category setting on the position. The exact interface can vary, so confirm the selected category’s collateral, eligible debt assets, LTV, and liquidation threshold before relying on it.

Does leaving E-Mode change my health factor?

Leaving E-Mode can change the parameters applied to your collateral and debt, so the health factor is recalculated and may rise or fall. Review the position under standard parameters before changing modes.

What is siloed borrowing in SparkLend?

Siloed borrowing is a separate, debt-side restriction associated with borrowing a designated asset; isolation mode instead limits borrowing against designated collateral. Support for these controls depends on the market’s configuration, so check the applicable asset settings.

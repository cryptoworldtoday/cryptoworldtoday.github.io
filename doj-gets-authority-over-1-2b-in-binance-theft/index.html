DOJ gets authority over $1.2B in Binance theft

US secured court authority to seize or restrain $1.2 billion of Binance's stolen crypto, while $2.7 billion is frozen and recovery efforts continue.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 23, 2026 · 12:43 UTC3 min read

The Justice Department said on Nov. 12 that it had obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a step that puts a portion of the funds under legal control while recovery work continues. The department’s announcement says the theft took place on or about Oct. 29 and was carried out by members of North Korea’s military, whom the FBI refers to as TraderTraitor.

The amount authorized is part of a larger pool: U.S. government agencies and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets, according to the Justice Department. The theft itself exceeded $6 billion; the Associated Press reported the FBI’s estimate at about $6.3 billion. The court-authorized $1.2 billion is therefore a substantial share of the frozen funds, but less than a fifth of the reported theft.

The distinction between freezing funds and obtaining authority to seize or restrain them matters. The broader freeze reflects coordinated work with virtual asset service providers to stop movement of the assets. The Justice Department’s announcement describes court authorization over a portion of that total; it does not say that the funds have been forfeited or returned to Binance or its customers. The AP reported that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

That leaves two measures of progress: how much stolen crypto can be immobilized, and how much can ultimately be recovered through legal proceedings. The $2.7 billion freeze is the broader intervention so far; the court-authorized amount is a more formal legal step, but still subject to the process that determines ownership. Neither figure establishes how much will reach victims.

The recovery effort also faces continuing movement of the remaining assets. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors are laundering the funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and that tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture work remains underway. Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued an alert attributing the theft to TraderTraitor and urging virtual asset service providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses used to launder the stolen Binance assets.

The FBI’s attribution makes this more than an exchange recovery case: the Justice Department says the attackers were North Korean military members, while U.S. officials have said North Korea uses cryptocurrency theft to fund its regime and weapons program. That attribution is the government’s finding, not a court ruling on the eventual ownership of the seized assets.

The next signals are the amount ultimately forfeited, any public accounting of assets returned to claimants, and whether investigators can interrupt further laundering through bridges, mixers and exchanges. Until those steps are clearer, the $1.2 billion marks court-authorized control over part of the haul, not a final recovery tally.

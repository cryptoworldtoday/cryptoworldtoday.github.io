US courts authorise action on $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department says courts authorised action against $1.2bn of Binance’s $6.3bn heist, as US and private partners report freezing $2.7bn.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 19, 2026 · 16:27 UTC3 min read

The US Justice Department says it has court authorisation to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, part of more than $2.7 billion frozen since the October theft. The Justice Department’s November 12 announcement describes the court-authorised amount as a portion of the broader freeze, a distinction that matters: the action does not mean the entire sum has been returned to its owners.

The department said members of North Korea’s military, identified by the FBI as the TraderTraitor group, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or around October 29. The Associated Press reported the theft at about $6.3 billion and noted that the FBI had previously attributed it to the North Korean hacking group. On those figures, the court-authorised amount is roughly one-fifth of the reported theft, while the combined freeze is a little over two-fifths. Both comparisons are approximate because the government figures are stated as “more than” amounts.

The nearer comparison is between assets frozen and assets covered by court authority. US agencies and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion, according to the Justice Department, but authorisation to seize or restrain applies to more than $1.2 billion of that total. Freezing can interrupt movement while investigators trace funds; court-authorised seizure or restraint gives the government a legal basis to pursue those assets. The two figures describe different stages of recovery, not two separate pools to add together.

That gap also leaves the ownership question open. The AP reported that the US government typically files a forfeiture claim before a judge decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance assets are continuing. Its announcement does not say that the court-authorised amount has been forfeited or returned to Binance or affected users.

The recovery effort depends on cooperation across the crypto system. The department says investigators have worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the funds, and that the alleged attackers continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are coordinating the recovery effort, according to the announcement. The FBI has also urged service providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses the group is using to launder the stolen assets.

That approach creates a different route to recovery from waiting for an exchange to reimburse customers: investigators seek to stop and legally claim identifiable assets as they move, while Binance itself is not named in the announcement as the party controlling the court-authorised funds. It also relies on providers and investigators being able to identify and act on transactions before assets move beyond their reach. The department says laundering activity is ongoing, so the freeze total may not be the final measure of what can be recovered.

The next signals are whether the government files forfeiture claims, whether courts determine who is entitled to the assets, and whether investigators can expand the amount under restraint. Further updates on the total frozen and any confirmed returns will show how much of the gap between the reported theft and current freezes can be closed.

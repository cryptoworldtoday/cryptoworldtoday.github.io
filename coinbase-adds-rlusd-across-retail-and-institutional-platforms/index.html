Coinbase adds RLUSD across retail and institutional platforms

Coinbase added Ripple USD across retail, Exchange and Prime on Oct. 28, widening access across three networks while global exchange collateral use remains pending.

By The Coin Wire EditorialOct 29, 2026 · 21:55 UTC3 min read

Coinbase added Ripple USD (RLUSD) to its retail app, Coinbase Exchange and Coinbase Prime on Oct. 28, giving retail and institutional customers another dollar stablecoin to trade and move across three blockchain networks. Coinbase says customers in supported regions can deposit and withdraw RLUSD on the XRP Ledger, Base and Ethereum, and convert U.S. dollars to the token at a 1:1 rate. Access to Coinbase Global Exchange, including using RLUSD as collateral, is still to come, according to Coinbase’s announcement.

The addition extends RLUSD’s distribution beyond the exchanges and onchain markets where it was already available. Ripple says the stablecoin launched in December 2024 and has since exceeded $2.4 billion in market capitalization. Reuters reported a figure above $2.5 billion on Oct. 28, citing CoinGecko, and described Coinbase’s move as an expansion of its dollar-backed token lineup. That lineup has included Circle’s USDC, PayPal’s PYUSD and Open USD, issued through a joint venture backed by Visa, Mastercard and Coinbase, according to Reuters.

What can customers do with RLUSD at launch?

Customers in supported regions can trade and manage RLUSD through Coinbase’s retail and institutional platforms, with deposits and withdrawals across the XRP Ledger, Base and Ethereum. Ripple says it is also supplying liquidity on Base to support use of the stablecoin there. These arrangements give users a choice of networks for moving the same dollar-pegged token, but the announcement does not specify fees, transaction speeds or regional availability beyond “supported regions.”

Ripple describes RLUSD as redeemable for U.S. dollars and backed by reserves that include dollar deposits, short-term U.S. Treasuries and cash equivalents. That reserve structure and the 1:1 conversion option provide a dollar-based alternative for trading and settlement. As with other stablecoins, access and stated backing do not establish how much liquidity will be available on each network or how frequently users will redeem tokens; those details will matter to customers choosing where to hold or transfer RLUSD.

How does the listing change Coinbase’s stablecoin mix?

For Coinbase, RLUSD adds a Ripple-issued option to a roster that already includes USDC, PYUSD and Open USD. USDC has been the exchange’s flagship stablecoin for years, Reuters reported. A broader selection may serve customers who want to use a particular issuer or network, while splitting trading activity among more tokens could leave each with less liquidity than the most established option. The listing makes RLUSD available through Coinbase’s channels; it does not, by itself, show how much trading or payment activity will move to it.

For Ripple, Coinbase provides access through a major U.S. exchange and its institutional platform, as well as a route onto Base. Ripple’s stablecoin chief Jack McDonald called the listing a “credentializing moment” in comments reported by Reuters. That is Ripple’s assessment of the commercial significance, not a measure of adoption. Coinbase’s own announcement frames the addition as another regulated, dollar-denominated choice for clients using its trading, settlement and custody services.

What should readers watch after the launch?

The next milestones are the Global Exchange rollout and whether RLUSD becomes available there as collateral, both of which Coinbase says will follow. Base liquidity supplied by Ripple is another concrete signal: its depth and persistence will help determine how usable RLUSD is on that network. Supported-region access, activity across the three networks and the token’s market capitalization will show whether Coinbase distribution translates into broader use. Until those signals emerge, the listing marks expanded access, while its effect on stablecoin competition remains to be measured.

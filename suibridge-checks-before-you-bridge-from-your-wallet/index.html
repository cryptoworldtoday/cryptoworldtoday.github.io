Suibridge: Checks Before You Bridge From Your Wallet

Suibridge risks before bridging from your own wallet: verify the site, token, address, gas and approval; save the Ethereum transaction hash to trace it.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 23, 2026 · 19:43 UTC5 min read

With Suibridge, you are responsible for verifying the site, token, recipient address, gas and approval before signing; confirmed transactions cannot be reversed. Use this checklist before you approve a transfer from your own wallet.

  • Official Suibridge URL: open it from a bookmark you saved after verifying the address, because lookalike bridge sites can request signatures.
  • Supported Ethereum token contract: confirm the contract, not just the token name or ticker, so you know which asset you are sending.
  • Sui recipient address: make sure it belongs to a Sui wallet you control; the bridge sends to the address you provide.
  • Gas on both chains: keep ETH for the Ethereum transaction and SUI for later moves on Sui.
  • Approval amount: compare the amount the wallet asks you to approve with the transfer you intend to make.
  • Transaction hash: save the Ethereum deposit hash so you can trace the transfer on blockchain explorers.

What am I responsible for checking before I sign?

Before signing a Suibridge transfer, you are responsible for confirming that the site, token, recipient and approval match your intention. On an exchange, the platform usually handles the destination details when you withdraw; from a personal wallet, you authorize the transaction yourself.

Lookalike bridge sites can ask for wallet signatures that appear to be part of a transfer. Start from a bookmark you saved after verifying the official Suibridge URL, rather than a search ad or an unsolicited message. A wallet prompt is a request to authorize an action, so read the token and amount shown there before approving.

When you are ready to transfer, use Suibridge to bridge supported assets between Ethereum and Sui. Make sure you are using the intended site before connecting your wallet or signing anything.

Token names can be copied, and a familiar ticker alone does not identify an asset. Match the Ethereum contract address against a trusted source for the token you intend to send. If the contract or approval amount does not match, stop before signing.

How does Suibridge move assets between Ethereum and Sui?

Suibridge moves supported assets by locking tokens on Ethereum and minting the corresponding asset on Sui. This lock-and-mint model means you need to check both the Ethereum token you deposit and the asset type you expect to receive on Sui.

That is different from an ordinary transfer within one network: your Ethereum wallet submits a deposit transaction, while the destination is a Sui address. Copy the recipient address from your own Sui wallet and check it carefully before confirming. A valid address can still be the wrong address, and the bridge delivers to the one you enter.

Plan for gas on each side of the transfer. ETH pays for the Ethereum transaction; SUI is useful for later moves on Sui. A successful bridge deposit does not mean you have SUI available to pay for a separate transaction after the asset arrives.

Timing and fees are outside this checklist. For this decision, focus on what you can verify before signing: the supported token, the destination address, the requested approval and enough gas for the action you are taking.

Why should I receive bridged tokens in my own Sui wallet first?

For a Suibridge transfer, receive the asset in a Sui wallet you control before sending it to an exchange. An exchange’s Sui deposit address may accept only coin types it lists, so sending a bridged asset straight there can result in loss if that asset is unsupported.

Say you normally deposit USDT to an exchange and want to bridge USDT from Ethereum. The ticker may look familiar, but the exchange still needs to support the specific Sui asset type you are depositing. Check the exchange’s deposit information for that coin type before sending anything there.

Receiving to your own Sui wallet gives you a chance to confirm that the asset arrived at an address you control. If you later move it to an exchange, first verify that the exchange supports the asset’s Sui coin type and follow its deposit instructions. ETH bridged to Sui also needs to be treated as the asset type delivered on Sui, not assumed to be interchangeable with every ETH deposit route.

This adds a wallet-to-exchange transfer, which takes another transaction and requires SUI for gas. The trade-off is control: you can check the received asset before deciding whether it is suitable for an exchange deposit.

What should I save while the transfer is pending?

During a bridge transfer, save the Ethereum deposit transaction hash and use it to track the transaction on blockchain explorers. Keep it with the token, amount and Sui recipient details you checked, so you have a clear record of what you submitted.

A transaction hash identifies the on-chain transaction; it is more useful for tracing a deposit than a note that you “sent USDT.” Confirmed blockchain transactions cannot be cancelled or reversed by the sender. That makes the checks before signing, especially the recipient address, important even when the transaction looks routine.

As you proceed, watch for the specific signals that matter to your decision: the Ethereum transaction is the one you intended, the destination is your own Sui address, and the asset you receive is the one you meant to bridge. If one of those details differs, do not make a follow-up transfer to an exchange until you have verified the asset and destination.

DBS adds 24/7 USDC access for Asian institutions

DBS and Circle will let eligible Asian institutions mint and redeem USDC with 24/7 dollar settlement, giving them a bank route alongside secondary markets.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 22, 2026 · 01:54 UTC3 min read

DBS and Circle announced on Nov. 18 that eligible institutional clients in Asia will be able to mint and redeem USDC through DBS, with dollar-to-token settlement available around the clock. The DBS announcement says the arrangement is intended to reduce institutions’ reliance on secondary-market liquidity for obtaining or selling the stablecoin.

The change is about access and settlement timing. Institutions can already trade USDC on exchanges and other secondary markets, but the bank route gives eligible DBS clients a direct mint-and-redeem path. DBS and Circle describe settlement as near-instant and available 24/7, removing banking-hour and cut-off constraints for this conversion. The announcement does not publish fees, eligibility criteria, minimums or a launch date, so the practical reach of the service is not yet clear.

Round-the-clock settlement, with access still limited

DBS links the service to treasury, settlement and liquidity management. For an institution operating across markets or outside conventional banking hours, being able to move between dollars and USDC through a bank could make it easier to manage those balances. But the announcement confirms direct issuance and redemption access only for eligible institutional clients; it does not describe a retail service or claim that every DBS business customer qualifies.

Using the bank channel also does not remove the trade-offs of using a stablecoin. The new route is an alternative to sourcing USDC in secondary markets, where CoinDesk reports liquidity and prices can vary. The partners have not disclosed the terms that would let clients compare the cost or speed of DBS’s route with an exchange, market maker or other provider. Access through a bank may suit institutions that want a banking relationship involved, while the service’s value for any particular client will depend on eligibility and terms.

DBS is building a second kind of digital dollar

The USDC service sits alongside a different approach DBS is pursuing: tokenised deposits, which represent commercial bank money recorded on a blockchain. According to CoinDesk’s report, DBS is connecting its tokenised deposit network with JPMorgan’s so eligible clients can transfer tokenised U.S. dollars between Singapore and the United States, including on weekends.

These instruments serve different networks and carry different arrangements. USDC is issued by Circle’s regulated affiliates; a tokenised deposit represents money held at a commercial bank. DBS’s support for both gives institutional clients an additional option, but the announcement does not say how the USDC channel connects to the bank’s deposit network or which use cases each is expected to serve. Nor does it establish that tokenised deposits provide the same public-chain access as a stablecoin.

Demand figures cited by DBS offer context, not evidence of usage for this new service. The bank says 88% of large corporations it surveyed are deploying or piloting tokenised-money use cases, and more than three in five are adopting or planning to adopt stablecoins. CoinDesk reports the survey covered 422 business and finance leaders across 11 markets. Those figures point to institutional interest, but neither source says how many firms will qualify for or use the DBS-Circle channel.

The next signals are the service’s launch timing and operating terms: which clients qualify, what transactions cost, and how redemption and settlement perform in practice. Those details will show how much the bank route changes access compared with secondary markets—and how it fits beside DBS’s tokenised deposits.

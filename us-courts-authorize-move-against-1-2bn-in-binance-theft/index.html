US courts authorize move against $1.2bn in Binance theft

US courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion from the Binance theft, part of over $2.7 billion frozen as investigators trace the funds.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 17, 2026 · 13:31 UTC3 min read

US authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a legal step in an effort to stop the funds moving and potentially recover them. The Justice Department said on November 12 that the amount is part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through combined government and private-sector efforts. The department’s announcement on the Binance assets describes the action as ongoing, not a completed return of the money.

The theft took place on or about October 29. The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from the exchange. The FBI had attributed the attack to the group before the seizure announcement. The court-authorized amount is therefore a substantial portion of the reported theft, but it remains smaller than the total said to have been stolen.

The figures describe different stages of the response. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen, while courts have authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion of that total. A freeze can prevent assets from moving; court authorization gives the government a further legal route to take control. Neither figure means that Binance has already received the funds. The Associated Press reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim before a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered assets.

The scale is unusual, but the mechanism depends on cooperation beyond law enforcement. The Justice Department says its teams worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the stolen funds. That cooperation can help block assets held by services able to act on a request, while investigators continue tracing transfers through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. The department says TraderTraitor actors are still laundering assets through those channels, so the amount frozen may change as tracing and intervention continue.

The difference between a freeze and a final recovery matters for victims and for exchanges. A freeze limits movement of identified assets, but it does not by itself settle ownership, complete forfeiture or establish when any recovered value would be returned. The reported $2.7 billion also combines government and private-sector efforts; the Justice Department’s $1.2 billion figure is the portion for which it says it has court authorization to seize or restrain. Those totals should not be added together.

The immediate measure of progress is whether investigators and service providers can keep identifying and blocking assets as they move between platforms and protocols. The Justice Department says tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts are ongoing. The next meaningful updates will be any further court actions, changes in the amount frozen, and decisions on claims to assets once forfeiture proceedings advance. Until then, the seizure authorization marks a legal and operational step, while the ultimate recovery remains unresolved.

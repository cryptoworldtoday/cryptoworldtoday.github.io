BlackRock opens Hong Kong tokenized fund with $100 million

BlackRock’s HKD fund opened with $100 million, pairing blockchain-recorded units and HKDAP settlement with a conventional money-market portfolio.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 24, 2026 · 20:25 UTC3 min read

BlackRock opened its HKD Digital Liquidity Fund to Hong Kong investors on Nov. 24, with $100 million in assets as of Nov. 20, according to The Block’s report on the launch. The fund offers conventional and tokenized units in the same Hong Kong dollar money-market strategy, bringing blockchain-based ownership records and digital-cash settlement into a product that invests in short-term instruments.

The launch extends BlackRock’s tokenized cash products into Hong Kong and Asia Pacific. BlackRock said in its announcement of the fund’s regulatory authorization that this is its first tokenized offering in the region. The fund received authorization in September; the opening and initial asset figure mark the move from approved product to live investor access.

What does tokenization change for investors?

The underlying portfolio remains a money-market fund, not a stablecoin. BlackRock says it invests in high-quality, short-term Hong Kong dollar instruments, including government bills and deposits. Investors can choose tokenized units, whose ownership records use blockchain technology, or non-tokenized units. The distinction changes how ownership is recorded and how eligible investors can transact; it does not replace the portfolio with crypto assets.

Professional investors can subscribe and redeem using digital cash, including HKDAP, a Hong Kong dollar-backed stablecoin issued by Anchorpoint Financial. BlackRock’s announcement says Standard Chartered will distribute HKDAP and act as the fund’s custodian, administrator and trustee, as well as handle tokenization responsibilities. This connects a regulated stablecoin settlement option with a conventional cash-investment product, while leaving access to that option limited to professional investors.

How does it compare with other Hong Kong cash products?

The fund’s distinguishing feature is the combination of tokenized and conventional units with a constant net asset value structure available to both retail and institutional investors. BlackRock described it as the first Hong Kong-domiciled money-market fund to offer that combination of CNAV access across those investor groups. The same fund still offers accumulating classes, where income is reinvested and the unit value fluctuates, alongside distributing classes that seek a constant unit price and pay income out.

Investors therefore have a choice between familiar cash-management access and blockchain-recorded ownership, rather than a choice between two different investment portfolios. The digital route may simplify ownership records and support digital-cash transactions. The conventional route avoids the additional technology, cybersecurity and wallet-related exposures that BlackRock lists for tokenized units. Neither format turns the fund into a bank deposit or guarantees repayment of principal.

What risks and signals matter after launch?

A constant net asset value is a target, not an assurance that the market value of the portfolio will never diverge from the dealing price. BlackRock warns that stress or large redemptions can lead to significant differences between the constant and market-based NAV, liquidity fees or redemption gates; in extreme circumstances the fund may move to variable NAV pricing or suspend dealings. Investors also remain exposed to credit risk on deposits and to risks in short-term money-market instruments.

The first signals to watch are whether assets grow beyond the reported $100 million, how much demand uses tokenized rather than conventional units, and whether HKDAP becomes a practical settlement route beyond the initial launch. Those measures will show whether the product’s digital rails attract activity that conventional fund access does not, while the fund’s asset mix, redemption terms and any NAV divergence will indicate how much liquidity the structure delivers in practice.

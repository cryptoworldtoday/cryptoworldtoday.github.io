JPMorgan Adds Solana Access for Two Tokenized Money Funds

JPMorgan has made MONY and JLTXX available on Solana through Morgan Money, adding a second network while keeping access limited to qualified U.S. investors.

By The Coin Wire EditorialOct 26, 2026 · 00:10 UTC3 min read

J.P. Morgan Asset Management has made its tokenized money market funds MONY and JLTXX available on Solana, giving qualified U.S. investors a second public blockchain through which to hold fund token balances. The firm said in its October 22 announcement that subscriptions and redemptions remain available through Morgan Money, its liquidity management platform.

The change extends the funds’ network options, not their investor base: this is not an open offer to Solana users generally. MONY is a Rule 506(c) private placement for qualified investors; JLTXX is a U.S.-registered government money market fund. The two products also differ in purpose. JPMorgan describes MONY as a way for qualified investors to earn U.S. dollar yields, while JLTXX is designed to support stablecoin issuers under the GENIUS Act.

What changes when the funds arrive on Solana?

Investors can now receive token balances at blockchain addresses on Solana as well as Ethereum. JPMorgan’s earlier May launch of JLTXX made it available on Ethereum; the Solana addition brings both JLTXX and MONY to another network. Investors still use Morgan Money to subscribe or redeem, rather than buying fund shares through an unrestricted decentralized exchange.

For JLTXX, the prospectus supplement filed with the SEC says the fund’s transfer agent keeps the official ownership record in a traditional investor register. Blockchain tokens can be used to submit transaction requests, while approved addresses are required to purchase, redeem or transfer balances. That means a public-chain token does not replace the fund’s underlying recordkeeping or make its shares freely transferable.

How does Solana access compare with Ethereum?

The expansion offers clients whose activity is already on Solana another network option for these same funds. But the filing does not describe a mechanism for moving balances directly between chains: it says interoperability is anticipated in the future and is not currently available. Investors should therefore treat Solana and Ethereum as separate available networks, not assume tokens can be bridged between them.

The public ledger brings visibility as well as access. The SEC supplement says issuance, redemption and transfer data for JLTXX token balances will be publicly observable, while identifying information linking balances to shareholders will remain in off-chain databases. Direct interactions with the fund’s smart contracts can also require investors to pay blockchain fees in the network’s native asset, SOL on Solana or ETH on Ethereum. For other transactions, the filing says the adviser or affiliates bear the fees.

What constraints remain for investors?

Eligibility and controls remain central. JPMorgan says MONY is for investors who qualify as accredited investors and qualified purchasers, and the new access is for qualified U.S. investors. For JLTXX, only fund-approved, allowlisted addresses can transact. That structure gives the fund a way to restrict access, but it adds onboarding and address-compatibility requirements that do not apply to ordinary permissionless token transfers.

The funds also retain investment and technology risks. JPMorgan says neither seeks to guarantee its $1.00-per-share value, and neither is a bank deposit insured by the FDIC. The firm lists potential blockchain delays, errors, security breaches, changing fees and network splits among the risks of tokenized fund use. The prospectus warns that a direct on-chain redemption request may be delayed and should be submitted ahead of the daily cutoff.

The next signals are whether Morgan Money sees adoption across both networks, whether JPMorgan adds further chains, and when the cross-chain transfers described as a future possibility become available. Until then, Solana broadens the rails for accessing the funds while leaving eligibility, fund administration and redemption processes in place.

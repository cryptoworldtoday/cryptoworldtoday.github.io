JPMorgan Extends JPM Coin to Ethereum Mainnet

JPMorgan has deployed its deposit token on Ethereum for institutional clients, widening its public-chain reach beyond Base while keeping access restricted.

By The Coin Wire EditorialOct 20, 2026 · 13:22 UTC3 min read

JPMorgan deployed JPM Coin on Ethereum’s mainnet on Oct. 19, making the bank’s dollar deposit token available to institutional clients on a second public blockchain. The move gives clients another network for moving bank money alongside tokenized assets and counterparties. CoinDesk’s report on the deployment says JPMorgan cited Ethereum’s liquidity and existing institutional use as reasons for the choice.

The expansion takes JPM Coin beyond Base, the Ethereum layer-2 network where JPMorgan first made the token available to clients in 2025. The bank’s Kinexys blockchain business already processes payments on a private network; CoinDesk reported that it handles more than $7 billion daily, against $12 trillion in daily payments across JPMorgan’s business. Ethereum adds a public-chain route to that mix, but it does not make the token an open-access asset: use remains limited to institutional clients.

What changes for JPM Coin holders on Ethereum?

JPM Coin represents dollar deposits held at JPMorgan, and its mainnet deployment lets eligible clients use that bank deposit on Ethereum rather than only on Base. The distinction matters: the token is tied to a bank account relationship, not simply a digital dollar available to anyone with a wallet. JPMorgan’s move therefore brings commercial-bank money onto a public network while keeping the participants within its institutional client base.

CoinDesk reported that Kinexys Labs head Emma Landriault described the expansion as giving clients “multi-chain optionality.” In practice, that means clients can choose between Base and Ethereum for onchain money movement. It also places JPM Coin alongside activity on Ethereum that JPMorgan’s statement cited as attractive: the network’s liquidity and institutional use. The deployment broadens the available rail; it does not by itself establish how much money clients will move there or how quickly adoption will grow.

How does Ethereum compare with Base and Kinexys?

Base was JPM Coin’s first public-chain venue, while Kinexys provides JPMorgan’s private blockchain infrastructure. Ethereum offers a different balance: it is a public network with liquidity and existing institutional activity, while Base provides a separate Ethereum-based venue and Kinexys keeps transactions within a private network. JPMorgan has said it plans to extend JPM Coin beyond Base; CoinDesk reported that the bank had also flagged the Canton Network as a possible destination.

The trade-off is between reach and control. A public network can put the token closer to onchain markets and assets, but JPM Coin’s restricted client access means it does not have the open participation of many crypto tokens. Its usefulness will depend on whether institutional clients can use it in the workflows they need, including payments and the settlement of tokenized assets. The bank’s reported Kinexys volume shows an existing private-chain business, but it is not evidence of JPM Coin activity on Ethereum.

What should clients and markets watch next?

The clearest signals will be whether JPMorgan adds the Canton Network, whether clients begin using Ethereum for transfers and settlements, and whether the bank reports volumes that let readers compare public-chain activity with its existing private network. The expansion is a change in where JPM Coin can operate, not proof that institutional finance has shifted wholesale onto public chains. The next test is whether clients use that additional access enough to make it operationally meaningful.

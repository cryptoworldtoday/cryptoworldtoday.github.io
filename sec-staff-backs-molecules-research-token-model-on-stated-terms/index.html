SEC staff backs Molecule’s research-token model on stated terms

The SEC staff’s conditional response covers LabsCo’s proposed token sales, separating research access and advisory input from any later, independently offered equity.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 26, 2026 · 14:44 UTC4 min read

The SEC’s Division of Corporation Finance said on Nov. 24 it would not recommend enforcement action over proposed sales of Molecule’s Project Tokens without registration under two federal provisions, if LabsCo follows the facts and conditions described by its counsel. The SEC’s response to Molecule AG makes this a staff position on one proposed structure, not a general clearance for scientific research tokens.

The timing is specific: Molecule’s lawyers submitted their request on Nov. 19, and the Division answered five days later. The response addresses Section 5 of the Securities Act, which covers the offer and sale, and Section 12(g) of the Exchange Act, concerning registration of the tokens as a class of equity securities. It says the position rests on Molecule’s representations; different facts or conditions could lead staff to a different view. The letter also expressly says it offers no legal conclusion on the issue.

Research access sits apart from company ownership

The model described in the request gives tokens functional uses tied to a research project: access to a project dashboard, records and publications, membership in a related association, and participation in community activities. Holders who lock tokens may be invited to vote on research questions, with one vote per locked token. Those votes are advisory; LabsCo retains the choice of whether to hold a poll and whether to follow its result.

That puts the token’s proposed role closer to access and participation than to a share. Counsel’s terms say tokens carry no claim to LabsCo’s profits, revenue or assets. Unlocked tokens may trade on secondary markets, but the request promises neither a particular market price nor a level of liquidity. LabsCo may mint or sell additional tokens, and Molecule and LabsCo would not operate a trading venue or act as market makers.

The comparison with a later equity raise is equally important. LabsCo might seek equity financing if research progresses and the company decides to pursue commercial development, but that would be a separate offering, subject to its own terms and registration or an available exemption. Token ownership or locking would give no priority in that offering, and a person could apply for equity without holding tokens. The two routes therefore serve different purposes: tokens provide the proposed research-community functions; shares, if separately issued, would confer ownership rights.

A narrow fact pattern beside earlier staff letters

As Crypto.news’ review of the submission notes, earlier Corporation Finance responses involving Fuse and DoubleZero concerned different token uses and transactions. Fuse’s request involved rewards linked to participation in an energy network; DoubleZero’s addressed defined programmatic transfers of its 2Z token. Molecule’s proposal instead joins research access and nonbinding input with a possible, separate equity process.

The common feature is the limited form of relief: each response concerns the facts presented by its applicant. Molecule’s letter does not establish that other research projects can use the same structure, or that a change in token rights, sale terms or marketing would receive the same response. Nor does it turn the underlying legal analysis by Molecule’s counsel into an SEC determination.

The first offer will test the stated conditions

Molecule said its first project using the model would launch within weeks, according to The Block’s report on the announcement. The project’s white paper and sale materials will show whether buyers receive the promised working features and whether the token is presented as research participation rather than a path to returns or equity. The next documents to watch are the first project’s token terms, the scope and reporting of advisory votes, any claims about future issuance or liquidity, and any separate equity offering. Those details will determine how closely the live product follows the facts on which staff based its response.

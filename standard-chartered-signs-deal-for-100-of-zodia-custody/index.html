Standard Chartered signs deal for 100% of Zodia Custody

Standard Chartered signed binding agreements for Zodia Custody, bringing regulated crypto custody in-house while its software business becomes a separate venture, pending approvals.

By The Coin Wire EditorialOct 21, 2026 · 15:55 UTC4 min read

Standard Chartered has signed definitive agreements to acquire all of Zodia Custody Limited, moving the custodian’s regulated operations into the bank’s Securities Services business while separating its software and professional services arm. The deal, announced on Oct. 21, remains subject to regulatory approvals and customary closing conditions. In its announcement, Standard Chartered said the acquisition will consolidate its digital asset custody activities and support a combined offering of traditional and digital asset securities services for institutional clients.

The agreement advances a process that began with a non-binding offer. Standard Chartered said in May that Zodia shareholders and noteholders had accepted that offer; the new agreements make the proposed 100% acquisition definitive, but do not complete it. The Block reported that financial terms were not disclosed. No purchase price or closing date has been announced in the sources reviewed.

What exactly is Standard Chartered buying?

Standard Chartered I H Limited will acquire Zodia Custody Limited, the parent of regulated operating entities in Singapore, Hong Kong, Australia, the United Arab Emirates and Luxembourg, according to the bank. After completion, the regulated custody business is to be integrated into Standard Chartered’s existing digital asset custody activities within Securities Services.

The transaction divides Zodia’s current activities by function. Its digital asset software and professional services business will be separated, with related assets transferred to Zodia Solutions, an independent entity to be established under SC Ventures. That company is intended to continue supplying bank-grade infrastructure to financial institutions, including Standard Chartered. The result is a bank-owned custody operation alongside a separate technology provider, rather than a transfer of every part of Zodia into the bank.

How does this change the bank’s position?

Before the deal, Standard Chartered already had digital asset custody activities, and Zodia operated as a separate regulated custodian serving institutional clients. The acquisition would combine Zodia’s regulated custody operations with the bank’s existing business. Standard Chartered says the consolidation is meant to create revenue synergies and a more unified institutional offering; those are stated aims, not reported outcomes.

The structure also leaves a role for specialist infrastructure outside the custody business. Zodia Solutions is expected to serve institutions building digital asset services and will have a number of bank investors, including NAB Ventures, Northern Trust, SBI Ventures, Further Ventures, Emirates NBD, Investcorp and SC Ventures, according to the announcement. That leaves the bank with custody operations while the separate venture can supply technology to institutions, including its new parent’s group. How independently it serves other firms, and how the separation works in practice, will become clearer after closing.

For institutional clients, the near-term distinction is between the ownership change and the service they receive. Standard Chartered said existing Zodia Custody clients will continue to be serviced as usual and that it does not expect disruption from the transaction. That commitment describes the transition plan; it does not establish how services or products may change after integration.

What should clients and competitors watch next?

The first milestone is regulatory approval and completion. Until then, the planned transfer of custody operations and the separation of software assets remain conditional. After closing, clients and competitors can watch whether Standard Chartered integrates Zodia’s regulated entities into Securities Services as described, whether the promised unified offering takes shape, and how Zodia Solutions serves institutions from outside the custody business.

The division of the businesses sets up two different tests. For Standard Chartered, the question is whether combining regulated custody with its existing platform produces a broader institutional service. For Zodia Solutions, it is whether a venture with bank investors can continue supplying infrastructure to financial institutions while also serving Standard Chartered. The agreement establishes the intended structure; approval, completion and the subsequent integration will show how much of it is delivered.

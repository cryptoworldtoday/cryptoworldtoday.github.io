Visa adds Monad as stablecoin settlement option

Visa has added Monad as a settlement option for participating issuers and acquirers, widening its stablecoin rails as annualized volume tops $25 billion.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 11, 2026 · 07:16 UTC3 min read

Visa has added the Monad blockchain as a stablecoin settlement option for participating issuers and acquirers, the Monad Foundation announced on November 10 in a release covered by The Block. The addition gives payment institutions another network for settling obligations with Visa; it does not change how consumers pay by card.

The move expands a program that already spans several blockchains as Visa’s stablecoin settlement activity grows. The Monad Foundation’s release put Visa’s annualized settlement run rate at $25 billion as of September 2026, roughly 10 times the year-earlier level, with more than 225 stablecoin-linked card programs live globally. That figure describes Visa’s overall program, not settlement volume on Monad.

How does Monad fit alongside Visa’s existing networks?

It joins networks Visa had already named for stablecoin settlement, including Avalanche, Ethereum, Solana, Stellar, Arc, Base, Canton, Polygon and Tempo. Visa announced the latter five in April, according to Unchained. On an October 27 earnings call, Visa chief executive Ryan McInerney said the company supported 10 blockchains, but did not name them; neither Visa nor the Monad Foundation clarified whether that count included Monad.

The network choice matters because settlement is a back-office process: it moves funds between Visa and participating financial institutions after card transactions. The Foundation says blockchain settlement can operate on weekends and holidays, unlike the five-business-day schedule it attributes to traditional banking rails. Visa vice president Dan Roesbery said the addition gives partners more flexibility to choose networks for stablecoin-enabled payments.

Monad is Ethereum Virtual Machine compatible, which the Foundation says can let institutions using Ethereum-based custody, wallet and compliance tools extend those integrations rather than build new ones. That may reduce implementation work, but the announcement names no issuers or acquirers that have chosen Monad, no stablecoins available for settlement on it, and no volume routed through it. Visa’s program-level growth therefore shows the scale of the broader activity, not adoption of this particular option.

What could make Monad distinct from the alternatives?

The Foundation says a planned feature called Private Settlement will let institutions keep sensitive transaction details confidential while making them available to auditors and regulators when needed. It is due to reach Visa partners in the coming months, so that capability is not yet part of the live addition. The design is intended to keep private transactions connected to liquidity elsewhere on Monad.

Privacy is also part of the competition among settlement networks. Visa has described Canton as offering configurable privacy for regulated markets and said Tempo is focused on private stablecoin liquidity; it has also tested private settlement on Canton with stablecoin issuer Brale, according to The Block. Monad’s pitch is to combine privacy controls with an open network and existing EVM tooling. Whether that combination suits institutions will depend on the feature’s delivery and their operational requirements.

Which signals will show whether the addition is being used?

The next evidence to watch is the publication of supported stablecoins, named participating institutions and settlement volumes specifically attributable to Monad. The timing and capabilities of Private Settlement will also matter. Until those details emerge, the confirmed change is an expanded choice inside Visa’s stablecoin settlement program; the announcement does not establish that partners have begun routing meaningful activity through the network.

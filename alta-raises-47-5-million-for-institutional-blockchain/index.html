Alta raises $47.5 million for institutional blockchain

Alta raised $47.5 million in a Series A led by Index Ventures, bringing total funding to $80 million for a blockchain built around institutional privacy and compliance.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 12, 2026 · 13:11 UTC3 min read

Alta announced a $47.5 million Series A on November 10, led by Index Ventures, to build blockchain infrastructure for financial institutions with privacy and compliance controls built into its design. The round brings the company’s total funding to $80 million, including $32.5 million in earlier seed financing. Alta’s funding announcement says the new capital will support product development, institutional partnerships and hiring in engineering and compliance.

The pitch is aimed at a specific gap: banks and payment companies may want blockchain settlement, but need controls over transaction privacy, counterparties and compliance. That puts Alta in competition with existing networks and payment infrastructure, while asking institutions to adopt a new chain rather than use platforms with established activity.

What does Alta’s blockchain add for financial institutions?

Alta says it is building privacy and programmable compliance into the blockchain itself. Its announcement describes an architecture based on recursive zero-knowledge cryptography, intended to let institutions keep balances, transactions, identities and compliance policies private while still verifying that transactions satisfy required rules. The company says the network is designed to work across assets, chains and jurisdictions.

That design differs from using a general-purpose blockchain and adding compliance processes around it. Fortune notes that financial firms already have options such as Ethereum, Solana and Avalanche for crypto transfers. Alta CEO Loong Wang told Fortune that those networks do not offer the privacy and compliance tools banks and other large financial firms need. The distinction is Alta’s stated product rationale; it has not yet demonstrated in the announcement that its controls will be easier or cheaper to operate than alternatives.

There is a trade-off in building a separate network: purpose-built controls may suit regulated users, but institutions and their counterparties would need to connect to Alta for those features to matter. The company has not disclosed a valuation for the Series A.

How far along are the product and partnerships?

Airwallex has chosen to build on Alta and connect its global payments infrastructure to the blockchain, according to the announcement. The planned collaboration includes stablecoin pay-ins and payouts and links to local fiat payment networks across 200 countries and 90 currencies. Alta describes Airwallex as an early design partner; the announcement does not say the planned connection is already live.

Alta’s first product is intended to help businesses reduce money held in prefunded accounts. The company says it will use tokenized assets to support just-in-time funding, foreign exchange at settlement and movement into and out of yield-bearing tokenized assets. These are proposed capabilities, rather than reported operating results. Alta also cites an estimated $4 trillion tied up in prefunded accounts globally as the problem it aims to address.

The funding syndicate includes GV, Blackbird Ventures, Square Peg and Capital 49 alongside Index Ventures. The announced use of funds is continued infrastructure development, more institutional partnerships and growth in engineering and compliance teams.

What should readers watch next?

The next test is whether Alta can turn its design claims and Airwallex relationship into a working service used by financial institutions. Watch for a launch timeline, details on which institutions connect, and evidence that the network can support the promised fiat and stablecoin flows across jurisdictions. Its privacy and compliance model will also need to show how institutions verify transactions without exposing sensitive information.

For now, the raise funds a bet on specialized blockchain infrastructure rather than proof that banks will move settlement onto a new chain. Alta’s progress will depend on whether its controls and liquidity tools address operational needs enough to justify connecting to another network.

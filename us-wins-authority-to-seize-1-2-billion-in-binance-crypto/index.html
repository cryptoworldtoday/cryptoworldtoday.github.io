US wins authority to seize $1.2 billion in Binance crypto

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance’s stolen crypto, within $2.7 billion frozen so far.

By The Coin Wire EditorialNov 18, 2026 · 14:03 UTC3 min read

The US Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a step toward taking control of part of the funds but not yet a ruling on who ultimately receives them. The department said on November 12 that the amount is part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through combined government and private-sector action. The Justice Department’s announcement puts the authorized amount alongside an ongoing effort to trace and forfeit the assets.

The FBI attributed the theft to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor, saying they stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or around October 29. The bureau had identified North Korea as responsible in an October 31 alert and said the actors were converting assets primarily into Ether. The Justice Department’s November announcement says the stolen assets are still being laundered through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and that efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit them continue.

The figures describe different stages of recovery. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen, according to the department, while court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of that total. A freeze can block movement while authorities pursue legal process; authorization to seize or restrain gives the government a further legal basis to act on the specified assets. Neither figure means that the full amount has been transferred to victims or finally forfeited. The Associated Press reported that a forfeiture claim would typically go before a judge, who would determine who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

That distinction matters because the headline recovery amount is smaller than both the reported theft and the broader pool of frozen assets. The Justice Department says the theft exceeded $6 billion and describes the $2.7 billion freeze as the result of public and private efforts. Its announcement does not break down how much each party froze, what assets make up the authorized $1.2 billion, or how much has been returned. The department describes the seizure authority as part of a continuing process, not a completed distribution.

The response also shows why action by intermediaries matters even when stolen tokens move across decentralized networks. US agencies worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict funds, and the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation have urged providers to block transactions involving addresses linked to the laundering. The department says actors continue to move assets through bridges, mixers and exchanges. These routes complicate tracing and create more points where funds may be intercepted; a freeze can stop movement at a cooperating service, but it does not itself establish a final owner or recover every stolen asset.

The next measurable signals are whether authorities obtain forfeiture judgments, how much of the frozen balance becomes subject to final seizure, and whether the remaining funds can be traced before they move beyond reach. The FBI’s October alert said the actors were converting assets primarily into Ether, while the Justice Department now says laundering continues across several types of services. The gap between the more than $6 billion reported stolen, more than $2.7 billion frozen and more than $1.2 billion covered by court authority will show how much of the recovery effort has advanced from restraint to legal control.

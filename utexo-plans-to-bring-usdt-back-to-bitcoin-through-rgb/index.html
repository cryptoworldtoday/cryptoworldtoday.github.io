Utexo plans to bring USDT back to Bitcoin through RGB

Tether-backed Utexo plans to issue USDT on Bitcoin via RGB this month, reviving the token’s original network while testing a more private payment model.

By The Coin Wire EditorialOct 3, 20262 min read

Tether-backed project Utexo plans to begin issuing USDT on Bitcoin this month, more than a decade after the stablecoin first launched there, according to CoinDesk’s report on the plan. The move would add Bitcoin to USDT’s current main venues, Ethereum and Tron, using RGB, a protocol for issuing assets on Bitcoin.

The return is a change in infrastructure, not a shift in USDT’s dollar peg. CoinDesk reported a market capitalization of nearly $190 billion and said Utexo has a commercial license to issue USDT using Tether’s trademark. The token first launched in 2014 on Omni, a protocol built on Bitcoin; Tether had announced plans to issue it on RGB in 2025, but Utexo’s plan is the latest step toward a new Bitcoin-based route.

How would RGB change USDT transfers on Bitcoin?

RGB uses client-side validation: transaction data stays largely between the parties, while Bitcoin’s ledger anchors ownership to unspent transaction outputs, or UTXOs. Tether’s 2025 announcement of its RGB plans described the intended benefits as private, scalable transfers and the ability to hold USDT alongside bitcoin in the same wallet. CoinDesk said Utexo plans to offer infrastructure through APIs, software development kits and cloud services for exchanges, wallet providers and payment companies.

Utexo lists three intended uses: private USDT transfers, direct swaps between bitcoin and USDT, and loans backed by native bitcoin without wrapping it on another blockchain. That differs from USDT on Ethereum and Tron, where account balances and transaction updates are recorded on public ledgers. The design offers less public transaction detail, though users and providers would need compatible RGB wallets and services to use it.

What control and adoption questions remain?

Bitcoin’s UTXO model also changes how restrictions could work. CoinDesk reported that Utexo cannot freeze an RGB asset in the same way Tether can freeze an Ethereum address. Instead, Utexo said it would blacklist UTXOs linked to sanctioned or illicit activity and share that information with exchanges and other providers; the affected outputs would become unredeemable through a bridge or minting tool.

The reported launch is still a plan, so the next signals are whether issuance begins this month, which wallets and exchanges support it, and whether Utexo follows through on its stated next step of extending USDT to Lightning. Those details will show whether RGB becomes a usable alternative alongside Ethereum and Tron, or remains an additional route with a smaller provider network.

Coinbase and Marqeta add stablecoin-backed cards for businesses

Coinbase will custody stablecoin balances and convert them to fiat for Marqeta-issued cards, adding an enterprise route to spend digital dollars across card networks.

By The Coin Wire EditorialOct 17, 2026 · 11:08 UTC3 min read

Coinbase and Marqeta announced on Oct. 16 that Coinbase will provide stablecoin custody, fiat conversion and settlement on Base for cards issued and processed through Marqeta. The arrangement gives Marqeta’s enterprise customers a way to build card programs funded by stablecoin balances, extending a relationship that already covers crypto-backed cards. Marqeta described the plans in its announcement of the Coinbase collaboration.

The division of work follows familiar card infrastructure: Coinbase handles the digital-asset side, while Marqeta handles issuance, payment processing, and bank and network relationships. Businesses will be able to fund programs around the clock, Marqeta said, and cardholders will be able to spend from stablecoin balances at millions of merchants that accept cards globally. The companies did not name a launch date, a stablecoin or an adopting customer in the announcement.

How does the stablecoin balance become a card payment?

Coinbase will custody the stablecoin balance and manage its conversion between digital assets and fiat; Base will provide the on-chain settlement infrastructure. Marqeta will connect the program to card issuance and processing, along with the banks and networks those programs require. The announcement does not describe the exact sequence or timing of conversion for an individual purchase, or specify which stablecoins customers will be able to use.

The model keeps the payment card as the customer-facing instrument while using stablecoins as the funding source. It does not mean merchants receive stablecoins: Marqeta’s announcement describes card spending at merchants that accept cards, while its earlier stablecoin partnership with zerohash explicitly said merchants would be paid in fiat. The Coinbase announcement does not spell out merchant settlement terms in the same detail.

That distinction matters because the card layer provides broad acceptance, while custody and conversion remain with the infrastructure providers. Businesses can offer spending from digital-dollar balances without building card issuance and payment processing themselves, but the arrangement also puts Coinbase in control of custody and conversion and leaves Marqeta responsible for card-program plumbing. The release provides no fee schedule or further detail on how those responsibilities will work in practice.

What changes from Marqeta’s earlier crypto cards?

Marqeta already issues crypto-backed cards for Coinbase, according to the companies. Coinbase’s 2020 description of its consumer card said cryptocurrency was converted to U.S. dollars before purchases and allowed users to choose stablecoins such as USDC. The new announcement shifts the emphasis to enterprise customers building their own card programs, and names Coinbase’s custody and Base settlement roles as part of the setup.

The proposal also sits alongside Marqeta’s July partnership with zerohash. That integration was designed to bring stablecoin infrastructure into Marqeta’s platform, with zerohash supplying custody, compliance and liquidity for on-chain movement. In the Coinbase arrangement, the named division is different: Coinbase provides custody, fiat conversion and settlement on Base. Both proposals pair stablecoin funding with Marqeta’s card capabilities; neither announcement establishes which customers will adopt them or how much spending they will attract.

What will show whether the partnership gains traction?

The next evidence will be a launch date, the first businesses to issue cards, the stablecoins supported, and the markets where the programs are available. Details on conversion timing, fees and the route from on-chain settlement to card payment would clarify the cost and operational trade-offs for businesses and users.

Until those details emerge, the announcement marks an expansion of Coinbase and Marqeta’s infrastructure partnership, rather than a reported rollout or measured adoption. The practical test is whether businesses use the arrangement to move stablecoin balances into routine card spending, and whether the resulting programs offer a clearer fit than Marqeta’s other stablecoin infrastructure option.
